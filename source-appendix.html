Each American aspired towards having their own miniature fiefdom: a grand castle (a single-family house), surrounded by beautiful rolling fields (a lawn), and fortified by a magnificent wall (a white picket fence). More profoundly, we wanted to have the space, the privacy, the freedom to be truly autonomous. This desire itself is not novel or intrinsically unethical. When we look to grand palaces, both ancient and modern, it is apparent that this desire has persisted across many cultures regardless of technological advancement or contemporary taste. But even innocent desires can lead to harmful developments. The desires which led us to develop ultraprocessed foods and social media were not intrinsically immoral, but we now understand how both have had clear and measurably negative impacts to our societal health and well-being. This same evolution from innocence to injurious is also present in the physical segregation of our housing infrastructure, which has fractured our communities. Today, “Housing Affordability” is arguably the most pernicious political football in Western politics. Across both sides of the aisle, politicians recognize the gravity of the issue, and while solutions are slowly becoming law, we need to think further than simply increasing access to the housing market. We have to address the inherent flaws built into the system of housing and transportation that has defined America for the better part of the last 70 years. As part of our Giving America series, we want to propose a holistic re-imagining of what cities should aspire to be in modern America.
