The Medieval Rise of the East

Just as Europe was at its lowest political and economic ebb, Arab merchants were particularly active in western India, with settlements in Gujarat serving as chief entrepots and avenues for Islamic penetration, from the late 7th century. This paved the way for the first Muslim conversions there – as well as for the accumulation of merchant wealth for which Gujarat would be known at the turn of the millennium. The Umayyad conquest of Sindh in 711, just as Arab troops invaded Visigoth Spain, accelerated such trends, to the point that Indian traders essentially abandoned the Arabian Sea routes, leaving them to Muslims.
