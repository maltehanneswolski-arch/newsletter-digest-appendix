THE NEXT NEWS CYCLE

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks at a midterm campaign rally in Rock Hill, South Carolina on Oct. 29, 2026. | Andrew Harnik/Getty Images

HAPPY ELECTION EVE EVE: America goes to the polls this week for midterm Congressional elections, and it’s time for the whole world to tune in.

Of course: Donald Trump will remain U.S. president for two more years and 11 more weeks beyond Tuesday’s vote, health permitting — but these elections will frame how his final period in power. Today’s Forecast sets out eight big reasons why the whole world should care about how a seismic week in U.S. politics plays out.

1. A REFERENDUM ON TRUMP: Determined to boost turnout among his supporters, the president has repeatedly asked Americans to pretend he’s on the ballot on Nov. 3. Expect critics to use that statement in the aftermath to hang Republican losses around his neck.

Trump still boasts almost daily about the size of his 2024 election victory. A seismic Republican defeat on Tuesday should in theory start to eat away at his political authority, given the salience of his war in Iran as an election issue — and especially as the clock ticks down toward the end of his term.

The unknown question is how far and how fast that process plays out, especially in Republican circles. As Forecast editor Charlie Mahtesian never tires of telling me, observers have been anticipating a Republican backlash against Trump for a decade. Ten years on, we’re still waiting.

2. REPUBLICANS CONSTRAINED: The Republican Party’s knife-edge majority in the House of Representatives has made for fractious negotiations in Washington — but it was still sufficient for Speaker Mike Johnson to chart one major piece of legislation through Congress, delivering tax cuts and a huge funding boost for immigration enforcement. If Democrats win back one or both chambers on Tuesday, it marks the death knell for the GOP’s legislative agenda.

But we also know this: Trump has shown he’s prepared to push what presidential powers he has to their absolute limits — and still holds near-total control over U.S. foreign policy. Diplomats from more liberally-minded foreign capitals say their chief concern is that Trump will be more inclined to throw his weight around on the world stage as he becomes more constrained at home.

3. DEMOCRATS UNLEASHED: Winning back one or both branches of Congress would give Dems control of powerful Congressional committees — and they’ve already made clear that a flurry of investigations would follow. The Dems have a long list of targets, ranging from Trump’s financial interests to the way he and Elon Musk tore through the federal government last year.

Of course: Trump and his allies are unlikely to be constructive witnesses, to say the least. But two years of bitter warfare with Democratic-led committees will suck up at least some of the president’s energy and attention.

4. AND IT MIGHT START WITH IRAN: “The most immediate impact globally of a sweeping Democratic victory Tuesday would be over the fate of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and the Iran war,” POLITICO’s Jonathan Martintells Forecast. “The last time a wartime GOP president suffered catastrophic midterm defeat was in 2006 — and then-President Bush immediately sacked his Pentagon chief, Donald Rumsfeld.

“If President Trump doesn’t fire Hegseth he may have a mess on his hands. Congressional Democrats have made clear they’re open to impeaching the Defense secretary, an inquiry that could become a de facto referendum on the war. That would serve, at very least, as a distraction as Trump considers restarting hostilities or pursuing a peace deal in the months ahead.

“More broadly, Democrats taking control of Congress would hand them the power of the purse. All federal spending comes from the legislative branch and that gives them a measure of control over not just the war, but all military spending and foreign aid. Past American conflicts effectively ended when Congress stopped funding the operations.”

But here’s a final thought: “There’s what Rumsfeld himself would call ‘the known unknowns and the unknown unknowns.’ Namely, how does a politically humiliated Trump act in defeat? He’s predictably unpredictable on the world stage. That’s the known unknown. But the unknown unknown about what lays ahead is humbling — and not a little unnerving.”

5. AND WHAT ABOUT UKRAINE? If Democrats take control of both branches of Congress they may try to push through new U.S. financial support for Ukraine, POLITICO’s global security reporter Paul McLeary reckons. “If they have Congress, they have the power of the purse. They could attempt to double or triple financial support if they wanted.”

The issue would be the presidential veto. “It’s hard to say if they’d have the numbers to get it into the annual budget,” Paul goes on. “There are Republicans in Congress who want to offer more support to Ukraine. We could well see that struggle for numbers playing out. We may even see Dems cutting a deal with Trump – offering money for his own defense projects if he signs off on money for Ukraine.”

What we most certainly would see, Paul adds, is much-increased scrutiny of the administration’s actions.”They can make life uncomfortable. They can pressure the administration, with investigations into the slowdown of weapons; into Trump’s games of footsie with Vladimir Putin. But they would not be able to make him fundamentally change course.”

6. AI SAFETY ON THE AGENDA: One domestic U.S. issue above all others is going to resonate around the world in the years ahead. “The future of global AI governance will start in these midterms,” POLITICO Decoded’s Calder McHugh tells Forecast. “How far voters are making their decision based on AI as an issue is up for debate; what’s not is that the new Congress will immediately be charged with trying to figure out how to keep America’s frontier AI models safe.”

Remember: Democrats are much more enthusiastic about AI regulation than their Republican counterparts, with a few notable exceptions. But as president, Trump holds veto power over legislation that does not have two-thirds support in the Senate — a threshold Dems are unlikely to be able to reach.

Which means it might be time to compromise. Trump told Fox News this weekend he is ready to become “Mr. Veto” if Democrats take control of both chambers. But he added: “At some point, we’ll probably make deals.” So what might an AI compromise look like? “The only thing that Trump might actually want that’s on the table is a bill that does some safety stuff while preempting state legislation,” Calder predicts. Expect the detail around that to be a key legislative struggle of the next Congress.

7. AND THEN THERE’S THE DATA CENTERS: It’s not just congressional seats up for grabs in Tuesday’s elections, but 36 governors’ mansions too. And these state leaders will play a key part in the rollout — or otherwise — of the AI infrastructure that much of the world will be using in the years ahead.

Remember: Data centers have become a proxy battleground for the broader political conversation around AI, and local leaders across America have grown far more cautious in supporting new projects as voter concerns have grown. A wave of Democratic victories in Tuesday’s gubernatorial elections could slow the rollout of this infrastructure — and so in turn slow the growth of the entire industry.

8. GET READY FOR TARIFF WARS: POLITICO’s economics columnist Victoria Guida tells Forecast she expects to see Democratic “pushback” on Trump’s tariff regime if the party regains control of Congress — though just how meaningful that proves to be is uncertain. “Congress has the constitutional authority to impose import taxes on goods,” she says, “and Democratic majorities in both chambers could constrain Trump’s tariff powers through oversight and funding bills.”

“But Dems are highly unlikely to achieve the margins necessary to roll them back entirely,” Vic notes. And the White House has already shown it is prepared to be highly imaginative in its use of presidential powers. Reuters reports that key Trump operatives like White House policy chief Stephen Miller are already considering how to flex the president’s constitutional muscles to stay one step ahead on tariffs.

FUTURE INTELLIGENCE

ON YOUR MARKS: Tuesday’s midterm elections will also sound the starting gun for the 2028 U.S. presidential race.

And that means the civil war that’s bubbled beneath the surface of the Democratic Party these past two years will come bursting to the fore.

Excitingly, we don’t even need to wait until Tuesday night to see the internecine warfare playing out. Look no further than the battleground state of Michigan, where Dem Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed is the progressive wing’s great hope of the current cycle. As Forecast has previously noted, a big win for al-Sayed on Tuesday would give leftist Democrats enormous succor as they try to secure the ultimate prize — a progressive candidate on the presidential ballot in 2028.

The primary battle that played out in Michigan earlier this year between El-Sayed and two much more moderate opponents, House Rep. Haley Stevens and state Sen. Mallory McMorrow, was already one of the most bitter of recent times. Stevens’ allies have sniped at El-Sayed for some time, and POLITICO scooped Friday that El-Sayed had told her to stay away from his closing events.

Today things got really ugly — with Stevens publicly endorsing El-Sayed’s Republican opponent, a man she had previously branded “MAGA Mike Rogers.” “Abdul said that I was no longer welcome in his campaign and that I should stay away. And he’s right,” Stevens said in a video posted to social media. “And that’s why I am endorsing Mike Rogers for the United States Senate today.”

Republicans are predictably gleeful, with President Trump himself firing off a Truth Social message within minutes of the shock endorsement, showering praise on the “respected” Stevens’ decision. Dems are in disarray, with Stevens’ long-serving staff quitting en masse and some Congressional colleagues discussing throwing her out of their caucus.

Whether her 11th-hour flip-flop is enough to actually swing the state back toward the Republicans remains to be seen, given the political headwinds. But as a sneak preview of the depth of bitterness between rival progressive and moderate camps within the Democratic Party as we head toward 2028, it will surely take some beating.

HARD POWER: CARIBBEAN HEAT

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham pictured at 10 Downing Street on Oct. 21, 2026 in London. | Alastair Grant/WPA Pool/Getty Images

Not content with opening one Pandora’s Box in British politics, new Prime Minister Andy Burnham is reaching for another.

Having just restarted the Brexit debate that dominated U.K. politics for much of the past decade, Burnham this weekend waded into a hotly-contested and long-simmering row with Britain’s Commonwealth allies over the nation’s slave-trading past.

Burnham will join King Charles III this week on the Caribbean island of Antigua for the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting. And this weekend, ahead of the trip, he broke with decades of tradition among British leaders by saying he was open to discussing a formal apology for the centuries of abuse committed by U.K. slave traders.

“We led the world in abolishing slavery — and we can show leadership again today,” Burnham wrote in the Guardian. While dismissing campaigners’ calls for the U.K. to pay financial reparations, he suggested he would be open to restructuring debt for the poorest Commonwealth countries, helping them repay debt over a longer period and with better interest rates.

The political reaction back in the U.K. has been divided, with progressives and liberals supporting Burnham’s intervention and conservative opponents bitterly opposed. It’s unclear exactly how the debate will now play out — but as with Burnham’s determination to put Brexit back on the agenda, the debate shows Britain now has a prime minister who actively embraces controversy.

SOFT POWER: ALL THE PRESIDENT’S SOURCES

After 55 years in the game, Bob Woodward — one of the reporters responsible for breaking the story of the 70s-era Watergate scandal that toppled then-U.S. president Richard Nixon — has once again gripped the attention of Washington’s chattering classes. His forthcoming memoir, the aptly-titled “Secrets,” unmasks a slew of sources who supplied Woodward with his groundbreaking scoops over the years.

There’s Brent Scowcroft and Lawrence Eagleburger, two top government officials who were sources for one of Woodward’s most famously-reported scenes: Nixon, deeply distraught, praying on his eve of his resignation. Then there’s Richard Darman, one of his closest sources — and friends — in the Ronald Reagan and George H.W. Bush administrations.

“The most celebrated source of my career was Mark Felt,” Woodward writes about his key Watergate informant in an excerpt, “the No. 2 man at the F.B.I. during the Nixon years, who died in 2008. I referred to him as Deep Throat. But if Felt was on one end of the source spectrum — a secret source for thirty-three years, who operated entirely in the shadows, deeply paranoid and careful about what information he provided me and how he did it — Darman was his opposite.”

The campuses are taking shape. The election will change the rules around them, but much of the 2027 pipeline already has a substantial head start. The fight is over 2028. Our model has 62.2 GW of US net additions that year, most of which hasn’t started construction. Most of it sits in states electing a governor on Nov. 3. This piece tracks the 13 states with the largest 2028 net additions in our model, setting aside Virginia and Mississippi, which don’t hold state elections this November. Korea’s Stock Market Crashed $1 Trillion. What Does That Mean for the Global AI Race?

View this post on the web at https://www.chinatalk.media/p/koreas-stock-market-crashed-1-trillion

Richard Gray is an analyst who works for a foreign policy think tank in Washington, D.C. Since 2025, South Korea’s capital markets have vacillated from the world’s best-performing to shedding more than $1 trillion in market capitalization. This volatility is a story of how Korea’s financial system overlays supply chain specialization. With Korean firms Samsung and SK hynix controlling a majority share of global memory, South Korea’s stock market functionally operates as a single, highly leveraged node within the AI financial architecture. Two interwoven dynamics explain the convulsions of Korean capital markets and their global ramifications. The first is the Korea discount, in which Korean equities are structurally undervalued because of lagging financial development and weak corporate governance. The second is a memory discount, with each leading high-bandwidth memory (HBM) company (Micron in the U.S., SK hynix and Samsung in Korea) having depressed valuations in their respective markets. The June-July 2026 crash rippled far beyond Korea’s borders and forewarns what may be to come in future market corrections. For Korea, its historically shallow capital markets and exposure to the memory sector — which is now facing emergent Chinese competitors — serve as overlapping pressures on its financial position. In this environment, the threat of a market correction cascading across global markets and the U.S.-China tech competition looms large. The Zenith: Capital Infusion The Korean market is essentially a bet on two companies competing in the same industry. Korea’s memory chip industry has driven exponential year-on-year growth in Korea’s capital markets. From March 2025 to June 2026, the KOSPI — Korea’s main stock index — rose 241%, adding nearly $3.3 trillion in capitalization and exceeding the total increase accumulated over the index’s 43-year history (Bloomberg). In June 2026, SK, Samsung, and Naver announced [ https://substack.com/redirect/19234011-d4cc-4e2f-907b-2e2387a94241?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] plans to build chipmaking plants and data center facilities worth $880 billion — a sum adding up to [ https://substack.com/redirect/44f4056d-b896-4aa1-854b-b786306795c4?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] more than 50 percent of Korea’s current annualized GDP. The Korean AI capital boom powered these commitments. In Q1 2026, SK hynix reported [ https://substack.com/redirect/afa4696e-47aa-4e02-9b38-d70935dc7f81?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] a quarter-on-quarter increase in revenue of 60%, while Samsung recorded [ https://substack.com/redirect/ddc5caef-cfa9-4644-bb53-bf28a9368cb7?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] 40% revenue growth. This concentration bleeds into the global semiconductor supply chain. As of Q2 2026, SK hynix and Samsung collectively accounted [ https://substack.com/redirect/1e5e732f-9940-497e-aeda-8c64dba1cb9b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] for 83 percent of the global HBM market and 63 percent of the dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) market. Micron, the closest American competitor, maintains [ https://substack.com/redirect/1e5e732f-9940-497e-aeda-8c64dba1cb9b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] an 18 percent market share in HBM and 24 percent in DRAM. This domestic and international centralization is one of the clearest financial expressions of the artificial intelligence boom. Alongside SK hynix and Samsung, Micron forms a memory chip triopoly. The emergence of CXMT — a firm with deep state backing from the world’s second-largest economy — introduces risks of China moving up another value chain, accelerating production, and drawing down market pricing. Such a conversion would challenge the position of the incumbent memory players, introducing new risks of shortfalls in valuation that could spread financial contagion across the AI stack. The Nadir: Capital Volatility Korea’s memory chip bet has since faltered. From June 22 to July 16, the KOSPI entered a bear market, shrinking more than a quarter and wiping out nearly $1.1 trillion in market capitalization. (CEIC) SK hynix and Samsung fell 38% and 29% over the same window. (Bloomberg) The June-July bear market was as tied to an industry as a country. Micron’s shares fell [ https://substack.com/redirect/34b0b7af-414a-479b-b236-1bd51ebfa398?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] approximately a third in the same span, while Chinese and Taiwanese semiconductor stocks also were being sold off [ https://substack.com/redirect/b97deaff-777a-476a-add1-e29927ad91d2?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ]. As the Iran war’s reescalation put additional pressure on the Korean economy through currency pressures and rising energy costs, Korean regulators warned [ https://substack.com/redirect/2b91cbd4-5f9f-48db-8471-bf8bf97d1ed3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] of chip-sector concentration as a market risk. As of July 2026, Samsung Electronics and SK hynix alone accounted for [ https://substack.com/redirect/40e014aa-739c-458f-999f-e64a522f175f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] more than half of KOSPI’s total market capitalization. In contrast, the Magnificent Seven (seven highest-valued American stocks) taken together are 37.2% of the S&P500. (Morningstar) Throughout the first half of 2026, foreign investments have flowed [ https://substack.com/redirect/0e714c0f-8177-4a7d-aeeb-2556e8df856a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] out of Asian equities at a record pace — with Korea among the hardest hit. In line with the KOSPI’s crash, in May and June, South Korea’s capital flight (in equities) exceeded $35 billion. On July 28, Korea’s stock market dropped [ https://substack.com/redirect/c4f700ff-8fca-45bd-9a2a-6fd3acdc019a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] more than 8% during intraday trading, the eighth instance that year. There have been at least 33 days of upticks and downturns [ https://substack.com/redirect/c4f700ff-8fca-45bd-9a2a-6fd3acdc019a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] of more than 5%. In response to the market’s sharp decline, the Financial Services Commission (FSC) suspended [ https://substack.com/redirect/e46a72dc-b3d2-408b-9bfd-422bf939911f?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] sales of leveraged Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) tied to individual stocks “until market conditions stabilize,” and tripled the minimum deposit required to trade them to $20,300, effective August 5. While ETFs often are an investment vehicle that holds a broad set of assets, the ones popularized in the Korean market were tied to the performance of single stocks and acquired [ https://substack.com/redirect/b97deaff-777a-476a-add1-e29927ad91d2?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] in high volume through loans. These ETFs let ordinary investors bet on a single company’s stock while magnifying gains and losses. After the ETFs launched in May, trading in SK hynix and Samsung and their ETFs came to dominate the entire Korean stock market, peaking [ https://substack.com/redirect/7efed865-3ec4-4fb1-80f6-44766ab677bd?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] at 84 percent of all volume in June. This volatility is partially powered by South Korea’s shallow capital markets. Historically, Korea’s capital markets have been characterized by a structural undervaluation of listed enterprises known [ https://substack.com/redirect/41a68866-eb63-4d86-a479-b4433931908d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] as the “Korea discount.” Financial analysis company MSCI continues [ https://substack.com/redirect/43cd122d-8d85-4935-ae8d-eb8d0e2be283?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] to label Korea as an emerging market, citing limited convertibility of the won in the offshore currency market, excessive requirements for foreign securities, and limited investment instruments. Korea’s comparatively low price-to-book ratio — a comparison of stock prices and a company’s net assets — introduces risks that accompany rapid capital market growth. Throughout 2025, Korea had the lowest price-to-book of any of the surveyed markets, well below Hong Kong, Malaysia, or Singapore. Era Dabla-Norris of the International Monetary Fund argues [ https://substack.com/redirect/a557583a-422a-41a4-8e40-f7292bf7d1d5?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] that shallow markets with limited diversification lead to concentrated counterparty exposures, potentially increasing financial stability risks during periods of market deepening. As the KOSPI retains a globally competitive return on equity — how efficiently companies generate profits from shareholder equity — the “Korea discount” is likely driven by a combination of genuine mispricing and governance risk. Caveats to the Memory Momentum Memory has become the highest revenue-generating segment in Korea’s capital market and the global semiconductor supply chain alike. According to Gartner, memory revenue is projected [ https://substack.com/redirect/83e638d3-c7c6-423b-a24a-e5e86bdc1c32?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] to surpass $1 trillion and account for more than 55 percent of all semiconductor revenue by 2027. The value of South Korea’s DRAM exports has increased more than fivefold year-on-year (CEIC). An increase in memory demand has led to Korea’s semiconductor export volumes more than doubling in that same period. At the same time, there is a global memory shortage. Samsung has earmarked [ https://substack.com/redirect/60880982-bda4-4c8d-8baf-ea5f02b80bb3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] 70% of its memory production for long-term agreements through 2030. This supply-demand imbalance is expected to become more severe through 2028 (JP Morgan). SK hynix CEO Kwak Noh-Jung has stated [ https://substack.com/redirect/a744ebee-dfcc-4fc7-a8e9-e4d9a77da7ae?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] that the memory shortage could persist through 2030. According to JP Morgan, DRAM prices rose [ https://substack.com/redirect/fd76d441-cb95-4227-a626-a10e38582fda?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] more than 400% from 2024 to 2026, with greater demand and long-term arrangements increasing overall memory pricing and reducing available supply. Historically, memory has been classified as cyclical, requiring intensive capital investments. Competition was primarily on the lines of affordability as opposed to quality, with an ebb and flow in demand and downturns shrinking the number of players within the market. There is an ongoing debate [ https://substack.com/redirect/c6fab2fa-6513-471f-8c45-e70c3670f049?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] on the extent to which the AI boom has reshaped the memory industry away from its cyclical nature. Unlike in the past, the market concentration among three incumbents — and CXMT as an emergent player — with the specialization and value-add of HBMs is thought to have transformed into a “differentiated product [ https://substack.com/redirect/c6fab2fa-6513-471f-8c45-e70c3670f049?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ].” Such a proposition relies on a continued and relatively stable demand to fuel the scale of ongoing investments. While long-term contracts provide immediate stability, they will also tie the memory industry’s future to the ability to extend them. Korea’s recent memory chip manufacturing investments are intended [ https://substack.com/redirect/34b0b7af-414a-479b-b236-1bd51ebfa398?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] to be operational in 2030, just as Kwak Noh-Jung predicts that the memory chip shortage may come to a close. What then? Maintaining the balance sheets and capital access of these national champions, therefore, is a much more expansive initiative than Korean financial stability alone. With CXMT looking to enter the HBM market, demand is not the only factor at play. On August 31, 2026, The Information reported that CXMT met its objective of producing HBM3E — the fifth-generation HBM architecture — with the intention [ https://substack.com/redirect/d00315ce-13af-422f-81c6-886755474181?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] of expanding production in 2027. This situates CXMT two generations behind [ https://substack.com/redirect/d00315ce-13af-422f-81c6-886755474181?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] SK hynix and Samsung, which have begun shipping [ https://substack.com/redirect/dea08400-2107-4cd9-80f6-35572739a6ed?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] samples of their seventh-generation HBM4E, and one generation behind Micron’s HBM4. What is unclear is Samsung and SK hynix’s timeline for mass production of HBM4E, as well as the extent to which CXMT will be able to leapfrog generations. CXMT has become a growing DRAM player and emergent HBM challenger. According to Reuters, Hefei “government-linked investors” and Anhui’s “provincial government-backed entities’ stake” account for roughly half of CXMT’s ownership, with industrial subsidies and state support contributing [ https://substack.com/redirect/706d1ca2-cd85-4bc6-bf5f-f9090bdb66d2?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] to the firm’s rise as China’s DRAM national champion. CXMT has continued to scale [ https://substack.com/redirect/f094e278-207e-48e6-948c-25cf2caa09e3?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] its market share in DRAM, growing from 3 percent in Q1 2025 to 10 percent in Q2 2026. In its first earnings report, CXMT reported [ https://substack.com/redirect/a733791c-698b-4012-9867-d85fa1108ea2?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] that its H1 2026 profit was $22.4 billion after sales increased by 874%. Its market cap, as of August 28, stood at 4.01 trillion Chinese yuan. In December 2024, the Biden administration restricted [ https://substack.com/redirect/706ab459-6120-4c68-9d22-b12c25b025f6?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] the export of DRAM and HBM to Chinese end users. In less than two years, CXMT has gone from prototyping the earliest generations of HBM to only two generations behind the cutting edge. This foothold within China — which remains its immediate priority, with HBM3E shipments to Alibaba and Cambricon Technologies — has facilitated its growing share of the global market. However, as Apple considers [ https://substack.com/redirect/07faf334-2ae2-4aa4-bd3b-9b12e6bbd6ac?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] using CXMT as a memory supplier — even as the Trump administration opposes [ https://substack.com/redirect/fd7f0403-e0b3-489d-980c-bbaead62b6d5?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] such sourcing — the existing shortages and price hikes could position Chinese suppliers to sell to American device and data center firms. This asymmetry of CXMT’s potential access to Chinese and global markets (assuming it is not added to Commerce’s Entity List), while Micron, SK hynix, and Samsung remain blocked from the Chinese market, could eventually compress margins and increase competition. This market landscape and historic cyclicality materialize as a “memory discount”, where memory stocks have lower forward price-earnings ratios (PE) — current share price compared with expected earnings — than other equities on their listed exchange. This is true for SK hynix and Samsung, which are lower than the surveyed Korean firms Naver, Hanwha Ocean, and Kakao, as well as for Micron, which has a similar PE ratio to SK hynix and Samsung despite the U.S. having significantly deeper capital markets. Of the memory players, the only current exception to this is CXMT, which recently listed on July 27. CXMT, in fact, outperforms Baidu, Alibaba, Tencent, and Xiaomi on a PE basis. As equities are one lever for companies to access capital, CXMT’s unique status as China’s only near-frontier player in a securitized, high-profit industry could provide it with unique, high levels of state support, reflecting in its valuation. When I previously posed [ https://substack.com/redirect/ea77db14-dde5-46ce-9b0c-40bc208badf2?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] a related question on the nature of China’s capital markets to Zongyuan Zoe Liu, Senior Fellow at the Council on Foreign Relations, she said that: “Importantly, the Chinese government has been encouraging long-term patient capital to be deployed in China’s equity market [for national champions]. One such example is the encouragement of insurance companies and pension funds to invest in the equity market.” CXMT’s market capitalization, therefore, is as much a financial position as national industrial policy. While successive Korean governments have attempted to close the “Korea discount”, this sectoral discount complicates the overall path to capital market deepening. Interestingly, however, the KOSPI bear market had an effect across sectors, with downturns in shipbuilding, chemicals, construction, and steel (CEIC). So, while the upside is contingent on memory performance, the downturn is cross-sectoral. Approaches to the Korea Discount For Korea, there are market, political, and fiscal incentives to deepen capital markets. Deepening capital markets is viewed to diminish price disruptions, which is amplified by Korea’s sectoral concentration. Improving Korea’s investment environment is fundamentally a political question, both in terms of credibility, [ https://substack.com/redirect/14445268-0714-4408-8e1d-77c73cbcbd01?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] enforcing government policies across political cycles, and deterring cross-shareholding structures that entrench [ https://substack.com/redirect/f57325a7-870c-407f-8259-8326a36fee7c?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] controlling shareholders. On the fiscal front, the National Pension Service holds [ https://substack.com/redirect/8339a4b0-a042-43fb-983e-d108fd64ec92?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] roughly 7 percent of Korea’s stock market capitalization, so any re-rating of Korean equities directly revalues the fund’s existing holdings. As Korean society ages, higher returns are necessary to ensure adequate future payouts. In response to a perceived valuation gap between Korean and international equities, successive Korean governments have increased [ https://substack.com/redirect/8afad4fc-15e7-4165-8634-089b11c0baf9?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] access to financial instruments and furthered corporate governance reform. Since President Lee took office, the following measures have been introduced or implemented: Financial Instruments: In July 2026, Korea introduced [ https://substack.com/redirect/0abaa7a6-e39c-4ee7-82f7-ee5e7f22d098?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] 24-hour foreign exchange trading, with an offshore settlement system intended [ https://substack.com/redirect/fd698fae-6288-4882-9a51-c1d86a162941?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] to enable registered foreign institutions to trade and source the won freely. Additional reforms include lifting [ https://substack.com/redirect/ba8c6751-6b35-4017-ac0b-38136d6a444a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] the prohibition on short selling and easing foreign investor access by replacing [ https://substack.com/redirect/0362cf2a-c251-438a-94f9-39b5b3cdf404?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] the Investor Registration Certificate with Legal Entity Identifiers. MSCI notes [ https://substack.com/redirect/2ed43373-2647-417d-8146-a7b476424580?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] it is tracking implementation, with the won not deliverable offshore and onshore liquidity during extended FX hours lagging developed markets. Corporate Governance: The Commercial Act was amended in 2025 and 2026, now requiring [ https://substack.com/redirect/d7c01446-0b11-489b-9846-c3e5ccebde70?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] directors to have fiduciary responsibility to all shareholders, prohibiting [ https://substack.com/redirect/8128082a-8fa3-476f-a0e4-895ab6f84517?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] companies from dual-listing subsidiaries, and restricting [ https://substack.com/redirect/378c8921-e8d9-4963-b713-911a675dabdf?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] the number of issued treasury shares and their entitlement to voting and dividend entitlements. Combined with dividend tax reductions [ https://substack.com/redirect/c503472f-3c50-4249-a22c-7d2aa15340f9?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ], reducing the maximum rate from 45 to 30 percent, these measures aim to increase capital efficiency and strengthen minority shareholder protections, reducing risk while improving performance. Where Korean Capital Markets Meet the World South Korea’s memory champions are a single node in an AI financial architecture. According to Gartner, in 2026 companies will spend [ https://substack.com/redirect/66c453f7-1807-4ce0-b642-460f295d5501?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] $2.6 trillion on AI (and nearly $3.5 trillion in 2027), more than 50 percent of which is for “AI Infrastructure.” With single-stock ETFs, memory scarcity, and concentrated exposure, Korea’s volatility has amplified to global effect. At the height of the KOSPI crash, Evercore wrote [ https://substack.com/redirect/598c43b0-51d1-456e-990c-1e8a85ea98cb?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] that Korea had become the “‘Tail’ that ‘Wags the Dog’ of global asset markets broadly”, with a correlation of “an astounding .95” between the KOSPI and Nasdaq 100. During this period, Leopold Aschenbrenner’s Situational Awareness lost [ https://substack.com/redirect/80e33274-7a5a-430c-ad05-e1440fefe041?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] 67%, with leveraged investments resulting in liquidation upon a market correction. The potential of AI as a technology has little relevance to the potential of an intermediate financial shock. Technology is not a unidirectional process of exponential growth, but of ups and downs where investments can underperform in the short-term and overperform in the long-term. The growth in capital expenditure does increase the overall risk of misallocation and valuation fluctuations if, at least in the short term, returns on investment do not materialize. As Washington Crossing Advisors writes [ https://substack.com/redirect/53bf992e-69ae-4506-99a1-53e92011018d?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ]: “The likely long-term winners will be companies that can run a marathon, not a sprint… They must fund innovation... and they must get through the cooling-off periods.” Firms that can absorb a market correction — or that receive sufficient government backing — therefore, may be positioned to capitalize on a downturn. Hedging Crisis in Korea Korea’s industrial investments reflect a commitment to maintain its advantages in memory. The $135 billion National Growth Fund [ https://substack.com/redirect/f7d6b905-a4b4-4334-a6cc-99fd9057947a?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] is a sector-focused industrial policy initiative, of which artificial intelligence and semiconductors are two of the twelve target industries. K-Moonshot [ https://substack.com/redirect/86ffe335-01c7-4318-9c60-c23d33a08e1b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ], similarly, is a $7.5 billion R&D platform, funding twelve projects to “aggressively integrat[e] artificial intelligence.” While these initiatives could strengthen Korea’s competitiveness and drive economic growth, how and where resources are allocated should be done carefully, with deterring market swings as the principal objective. Moving forward, Korea requires sustainable equity growth by hedging against risk — with the understanding that these are marginal efforts that may not overcome global financial trends. This can be pursued through two joint efforts of diversification. The first effort is constraining access to leveraged investment instruments. Korea’s stock market is a de facto semiconductor ETF, and the trading of single-company, two-company, or single-industry ETFs should be banned indefinitely. The second effort is broadening the market share of alternate sectors. While the June-July bear market indicates that downturns affected a broad set of sectors, greater KOSPI diversification would reduce the contagion effect of memory upswings and downturns. As one example, Korea’s defense sector may diminish sectoral reliance. According to Dina Ting of Franklin Templeton, Korea’s dual exposure [ https://substack.com/redirect/7d7a0c47-c403-4a50-a11d-d2b3b665e4f4?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] to AI and defense “helps explain why South Korea’s recent rally may be more durable than in past cycles.” From 2021-25, Korea accounted [ https://substack.com/redirect/90b7d21d-d46c-4c19-ac0c-a11b69d29178?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] for 3 percent of global arms sales, a 24 percent increase relative to 2016-20. South Korea’s growth in defense arms sales covered Middle Eastern, European, and Indo-Pacific theaters, representing 47 percent of Poland’s weapons imports, 10 percent of the UAE’s, and 42 percent of the Philippines’. This defense sector growth is likely to continue. At the Seoul International Aerospace and Defense Exhibition 2025, President Lee stated [ https://substack.com/redirect/7467dec2-3a1b-4555-9db3-30c35b82f4df?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] that it was his goal for Korea to become “one of the top four powerhouses in the defense industry.” As the Korean defense industry develops, the Financial Services Commission and the Ministry of National Defense should coordinate on a listing incentive program to bring emerging medium-sized defense enterprises to market, increasing the market share of non-chip sector companies over time.

As Korea capitalizes on strategic industries, prioritizing these emergent spaces — including, but not limited to defense, chemicals, robotics, etc. — to dilute any sectoral impact is crucial. Globally Integrated Shocks Historical precedents are pervasive in Korean financial thinking. Discussing the proposed $350 billion package of Korean investment in the United States, President Lee warned [ https://substack.com/redirect/05ef9832-4fa2-4bb6-8b45-99bd2110d590?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] that without a currency swap, “South Korea would face a situation as it had in the 1997 financial crisis.” More recently, he turned the same historical lens on the domestic property market, cautioning [ https://substack.com/redirect/a33ebf05-74ba-4a86-988b-095a490af995?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] that Korea may be “racing toward” the kind of real estate “bubble” that preceded Japan’s “lost 30 years.” Once again, Korea sits at the center of transnational financial instability. Looking ahead, McKinsey [ https://substack.com/redirect/f18acbd2-79c0-4386-967a-f0d0929f34d5?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ]projects [ https://substack.com/redirect/f18acbd2-79c0-4386-967a-f0d0929f34d5?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] that support for AI-related data center capacity could require upwards of $8 trillion by 2030, of which memory chips are a key pillar. Korea’s stock market is simultaneously shallow (thereby volatile) and concentrated (thereby vulnerable to market cycles and emergent competitors). With Korea’s position in the AI supply chain and the scale of capex, these questions are worth considering: What is to happen to these investments if another, more than $1 trillion shock hit the Korean market? What about the asset management firms that hold Korean equities — and those that bought in the preceding bear market? While SK and Samsung have memory shortages and backfilled orders through the end of the decade, they are continuing to expand capacity. What would happen to their balance sheets if memory re-commoditized, CXMT became a strong HBM competitor, and prices collapsed? As it begins its HBM drive, what would expanded state support of CXMT mean? If Micron, SK, and Samsung are sold for a “memory discount”, can Chinese industrial policy induce a premium to the national champion in a manner that neither the United States nor Korea could replicate? Conversely, what would the ramifications be for the financial positioning of Micron, SK hynix, and Samsung if CXMT continues to gain market share at competitive prices in legacy DRAM, even if they don’t advance to the frontier of HBM? American frontier labs have high valuation and low revenue; Chinese firms have suppressed [ https://substack.com/redirect/844df28f-9a11-4f28-a9ef-3e64992d286b?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] valuations due to state support; and Korean memory producers have exponentially growing revenue yet carry a discount. What, if anything, would cause these dynamics to flip? The technological and financial scale of these questions — and the people, countries, and enterprises embedded within them — is enormous. While these questions are not immediately answerable, it is vital for decision-makers to collectively confront their implications, assess the risk factors, and address existing vulnerabilities. The resilience of an allied AI stack, global financial stability, and prosperous economies all depend on it. There is also a human story to this volatility. For The Wall Street Journal, Jiyoung Sohn, Sooyoung Rhee, and Jack Pitcher provided [ https://substack.com/redirect/249774e0-9288-4527-b508-a9ba87c5bbcc?j=eyJ1IjoiNnhha3A0In0.Jik053lvvE-Ofpv9cpN9G_-RUPbW_JKjUogath2oHFk ] a snapshot into the “victims” of the KOSPI downturn. This is people’s livelihoods evaporating in an instant, with overleveraged, single-company and single-industry ETFs being legalized and encouraged at a time when Korea’s capital markets had not deepened. Such a system was poorly conceived, with the margin-called retail investors — from teachers to accountants to software engineers — unable to play the long game weathered by financial institutions. As AI investments continue to climb globally, a downturn “loser base” will expand from retail investors to multinationals, nation-states, and institutional investors.
