The Sordid World of Ottoman Muslim Slavery

One of the most puzzling tendencies in online discussion is to make excuses for Islamic slavery — the biggest, longest-lasting, most prevalent form of slavery in human history — on the grounds that it allegedly was more compassionate than other kinds. This is absurd on many levels; for example, the idea that enslaving blacks in Africa to castrate them and work them to death is somehow more virtuous than chattel slavery in the Americas, however horrible, is remarkable. Just to clarify the point, however, let me have a close look at one specific type of Islamic slavery that has nothing to do with Africa: the “devshirme” (“collection of youths”) kidnapping of boys and girls on which the Ottoman state based its growth and development. A lot of people have written about devshirme implying that it was some sort of unique, colorful Ottoman custom with no parallel anywhere else. This is absurd. For centuries even before Osman, the Ottoman dynasty founder, was born Turkic military dynasties from Egypt to India replenished themselves via the systematic kidnapping, sale and training of young boys, at first overwhelmingly steppe Turks and later overwhelmingly European or Caucasian (from the Caucasus region). The rise of Genghis’ Mongols in the early 13th century had many significant effects on Eurasia, and one of the biggest was the opening of slave steppe markets to loosely populated Turkic statelets in Anatolia, the core of modern Turkey. With the removal of Russian principates crushed by the Mongols and enthusiastic logistics provided by merchants in Venice and Genoa, the peninsula filled with slave-soldiers captured by the Mongols in the steppes, helping to reinforce their forces at a key juncture, just as Byzantium’s manpower was at its lowest following decades of civil wars and the loss of important provinces. Prominent field commanders were allocated feudal “iqta” lands (“dirlik” or “livings” in Turkish) the rents on which would be enough to pay for a number of well-armed, professional horsemen serving the state.
