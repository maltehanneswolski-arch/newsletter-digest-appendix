The Great European Migrations

The massive migrations in Europe between the 7th and 8th centuries did much to create a new landscape in which Slavic newcomers arrived from the East mixed and matched with older populations settled across the center and south of the continent. A good example of such penetration is evident in the strategic region of Moravia. This old Germanic stronghold was a thickly forested highland carved in the center by the Morava River – one of the largest tributaries of the Danube, flowing north to south providing a transport corridor linking with the Polish plains – and soon became one the main centers for Slavic settlement. Moravia had a sort of rough-and-tumble Slavic royal court by the 8th century. To the south, Slavs also flooded into the former Roman lands of Carinthia and Dalmatia, as well as the entire central Balkan region later known as Yugoslavia (or “South Slavia”). While many Latin speakers took refuge in the ports along the Dalmatian coast, the only non-Slavs who remained in any significant numbers in the interior were Albanians.
