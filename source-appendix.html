Age of AI Empires?

. Age of AI Empires? Will AI produce a new imperial age? In a new Foreign Affairs essay, Niall Ferguson, Lisa Klaassen and Eric Schmidt argue ( https:// ~~/AAQRx RA~/ ~~ ) it will. The US and China—the world’s two superpowers and its two leaders in AI development—may jockey for supremacy, but the bigger point is that AI could recreate broader imperial dynamics from history. “AI is imperial in two ways,” they write. “On the one hand, China and the United States had empire-like qualities before the AI race began … For both, AI is a way to extend the power they already wield. But AI is also by its nature an imperial substance. Whoever produces and controls frontier models acquires global leverage over innovation, production, science, culture, and, ultimately, advanced thought. Much as silver underwrote Habsburg Spain and oil defined the ‘American century,’ this new commodity will not merely enrich the powers that dominate it but also provide the basic operating system on which everyone else must depend.” Given that, AI-centered competition between the US and China sounds all-encompassing. But apart from that competition, AI also recreates dynamics between empires and the faraway places they sought to populate and control, the Foreign Affairs authors argue. Critical minerals necessary for microchips and data centers come from the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Indonesia, they note. As early as 2024, researchers warned ( https:// ~~/AAQRx RA~/ ~~ ) that AI development depended partly on human moderation and data handling, done in some cases by low-paid workers in developing countries. The Foreign Affairs authors call this “the plantation economics of the chatbot age. Raw inputs are extracted from the periphery, refined and assembled in the metropole, and sold back at a markup.” While competing with each other, the US and China will need to sustain themselves as they progress toward even greater global power through AI development. In a new paper, we propose five domestic readiness tests to complement the Maastricht criteria. We conclude that Hungary is not yet ready to adopt the euro.
