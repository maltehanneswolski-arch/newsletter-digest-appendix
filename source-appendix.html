As a drier, colder weather pushed nomads south, into somebody else’s lands, nearby tribes came together for better protection. The settlements were oval and of various sizes: typically, of less than 15 hectares, with some bigger than 100 hectares and three super-towns, all within twenty kilometers of one another, between 250 and 450 hectares – the largest now called Talyanki – and all inhabited simultaneously by over 5,000 people each, as if the product of a large confederation of tribes acting in concert. Defensive concentration was a temporary strategy, though, since all the big towns were abandoned by 3300 BC, as the descendants of their inhabitants scattered west and east as soon as it was safe to move outside from the uncomfortable grip of the power-mongers in the steppe towns.
