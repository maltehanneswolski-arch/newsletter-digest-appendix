Your daily briefing on SOUTH KOREA Executive Briefing Future Response Fund would give Seoul more control over future tax windfalls John Lee South Korea’s Ministry of Budget and Planning (MBP) on Friday unveiled plans for a Future Response Fund that would capture internal tax revenue above its long-term trend and channel the money into youth programs, growth industries including artificial intelligence (AI), regional development and education and talent. The fund would also hold reserves that the government could draw on when tax revenue weakens, with the trend calculated using the average growth of internal tax receipts over the previous 10 years. The proposal would also change how revenue windfalls flow to local governments and education offices. Seoul would retain the 19.24% local allocation tax rate but exclude money transferred to the new fund from its tax base, while replacing the education grant’s current 20.79% linkage with a formula reflecting nominal growth and part of the change in the school-age population. Money redirected from schools would remain earmarked for education and talent programs. Why it matters The overhaul reflects two pressures on South Korea’s finances. Semiconductor cycles cause large swings in corporate tax receipts, allowing spending to surge during booms before revenue weakness forces restraint, while the education formula has continued to increase funding despite demographic decline. The school-age population fell 32.8% between 2010 and 2025, while the education grant rose 117.6%. The changes would give Seoul greater influence over how future tax windfalls are spent. Local governments would receive less of the extra revenue generated during tax booms through existing transfer formulas and could instead compete for projects financed through the fund’s regional account, while universities, early-childhood education and talent programs would gain access to money previously directed mainly toward schools. The fund would also give the government a more flexible financing channel for AI and other strategic industries, increasing the importance of rules governing project selection and legislative oversight. The government will hold a legislative consultation from Aug. 24-28, take the package to the Cabinet on Sept. 1 and submit it to the National Assembly with the 2027 budget on Sept. 3.
